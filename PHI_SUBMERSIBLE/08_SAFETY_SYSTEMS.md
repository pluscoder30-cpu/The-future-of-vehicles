# PHI SUBMERSIBLE — Safety Systems

## Safety Philosophy
Underwater operations carry inherent risks: pressure, drowning, entrapment, and equipment failure. The Submersible's safety system addresses each with redundant, independent protections.

## Multi-Layer Safety Architecture

### Layer 1: Hull Integrity
- Triple O-ring seals on all penetrations
- Hull thickness rated to 3x maximum depth (500m)
- Implosion detection sensors
- Automatic pressure compensation
- Hull breach alert and emergency ascent

### Layer 2: Buoyancy Safety
- Variable buoyancy system (normal control)
- Drop weights (emergency positive buoyancy)
- Compressed air blow (surface recovery)
- Buoyancy check before every dive
- Automatic weight drop on hull breach

### Layer 3: Life Support
- 2-hour emergency oxygen reserve
- CO2 scrubbing system
- Temperature regulation
- Humidity control
- Emergency surface breathing apparatus

### Layer 4: Emergency Systems
- Emergency ascent (10 m/s for 30 seconds)
- 433 MHz beacon (surface)
- Acoustic distress signal (underwater)
- GPS location broadcast
- Strobe light (surface)

## Harness System
- 2-point quick-release harness per occupant
- Single-lever release for emergency exit
- Load-rated to 15 kN per attachment point
- Auto-release on surface arrival

## Depth Safety
- **Maximum Rated Depth:** 500m
- **Safety Factor:** 3x (hull rated to 1500m)
- **Deco Stops:** Not required (no compressed air)
- **Ascent Rate:** 10 m/s emergency, 3 m/s normal
- **Bottom Time:** Unlimited (battery limited)

## Emergency Egress
1. Surface (emergency ascent)
2. Equalize pressure (hatch opens at 1 bar)
3. Open hatch (hydraulic assist)
4. Exit to surface
5. Activate beacon
6. Await rescue

## Inspection Protocol
- Pre-dive visual inspection (5 minutes)
- Automated self-test on power-up (2 minutes)
- Hull integrity check (pressure test)
- Ballast system test
- Life support verification
- Communication test
