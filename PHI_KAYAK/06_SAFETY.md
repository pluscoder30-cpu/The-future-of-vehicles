# PHI_KAYAK — Safety Precautions

## CRITICAL SAFETY WARNINGS

> ⚠️ **READ ALL WARNINGS BEFORE OPERATING THE KAYAK**
> ⚠️ **FAILURE TO FOLLOW SAFETY RULES CAN RESULT IN DROWNING OR DEATH**

---

## Water Safety

### Drowning Risk
- **ALWAYS wear a PFD (life jacket)** — non-negotiable
- **Never paddle alone** — always have a buddy
- **Tell someone where you're going** and when you'll return
- **Check weather before every trip** — never paddle in storms
- **Know your limits** — don't go beyond your skill level
- **Stay hydrated** — bring water even on short trips

### Cold Water Risk
- Water below 20°C (68°F) can cause cold water shock
- Symptoms: gasping, hyperventilation, loss of muscle control
- **Wear a wetsuit or drysuit** in cold water
- **Know the signs of hypothermia**
- **Get out of water immediately** if shivering

### Current & Tide Risk
- **Never paddle in strong currents** without training
- **Check tide schedules** for coastal paddling
- **Stay away from dam releases** and rapids
- **If caught in current**, paddle parallel to shore, not against it

---

## Electrical Safety

### Battery Hazards
- **LiFePO4 batteries are safer than Li-Ion** but still store energy
- Never expose battery to temperatures above 60°C (140°F)
- Never puncture, crush, or disassemble battery cells
- If battery swells, smells, or smokes — **STOP USE IMMEDIATELY**
- **Charge only with the supplied charger** — never use other chargers
- Charge in a well-ventilated area
- Unplug charger when fully charged

### Water + Electricity
- All electronics are **waterproof** but not waterproof forever
- **Never submerge battery box** intentionally
- **Check seals before every trip** — dry out completely after each use
- **If electronics get wet**, disconnect battery immediately
- **Never work on electronics with wet hands**

### Motor Safety
- Motor can cause **serious injury** if fingers get caught in impeller
- **Never touch impeller while motor is running**
- **Turn off motor** before removing paddle
- **Keep hands away from intake grate** — suction can trap fingers

## Kayak Safety

### Capsizing Risk
- **Know how to re-enter kayak** from water (practice in calm water first)
- **Stay calm** if you capsize — hold onto kayak
- **Keep paddle and PFD attached** with leashes
- **Never stand up in kayak** — always stay seated

### Weight Limit
- Maximum total weight: 150 kg (330 lbs) — kayak + person + gear
- **Exceeding weight limit** reduces stability and increases sinking risk
- **Distribute weight evenly** — heavy items low and centered
- **Don't overload stern** — keeps bow up for better tracking

### Navigation
- **Stay in shallow water** (< 3m depth) if inexperienced
- **Stay away from boat traffic** — they may not see you
- **Use navigation light** when paddling near dawn/dusk
- **Carry whistle** for signaling
- **Know right-of-way rules** — kayaks yield to boats

---

## Emergency Procedures

### Capsizing
1. **Stay calm** — hold onto kayak and paddle
2. **Get back in kayak** if possible (re-entry technique)
3. **If can't re-enter**, hold onto kayak and kick to shore
4. **Signal for help** if needed (whistle, waving)
5. **If in cold water**, get out ASAP — hypothermia risk

### Motor Failure
1. **Stop motor** (turn off throttle)
2. **Remove paddle** from holder (disables motor)
3. **Use paddle** to propel kayak manually
4. **Paddle to shore** or safe area
5. **Check motor** only when safely on shore

### Battery Emergency
1. If battery smokes or catches fire: **DO NOT USE WATER**
2. **Capsize kayak** if necessary to submerge battery
3. **Move away from kayak** if possible
4. **Call for help** (911 or marine radio)
5. **If skin contact with battery acid**, flush with water

### Lost Paddle
1. **Don't panic** — you have motor
2. **Use motor** to paddle back
3. **If motor also fails**, use hands/arms to paddle
4. **Stay with kayak** — it's a flotation device
5. **Signal for help** if needed

## Weather Limitations

| Condition | Status | Action |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Wind < 15 km/h | ✅ Safe | Normal paddling |
| Wind 15-25 km/h | ⚠️ Caution | Stay close to shore |
| Wind > 25 km/h | ❌ Unsafe | Do not paddle |
| Rain (light) | ⚠️ Caution | Stay close to shore |
| Rain (heavy) | ❌ Unsafe | Do not paddle |
| Thunderstorm | ❌ Unsafe | Do not paddle |
| Fog | ❌ Unsafe | Do not paddle |
| Waves > 0.5m | ⚠️ Caution | Experienced paddlers only |

## Legal Requirements

### United States
- PFD required for all ages (USCG regulation)
- Navigation light required after sunset
- Whistle required on boats (including kayaks)
- Kayak must be registered if motorized (check state laws)
- **Check state laws for motorized kayak requirements**

### International
- **Canada**: PFD required, check provincial motor laws
- **United Kingdom**: PFD recommended, check local laws
- **Australia**: PFD required in many states, check local laws
