# PHI_WATER_HOVERCRAFT — Regulatory Status

## Legal Classification

The phi-harmonic hovercraft is classified as a **personal watercraft (PWC)** or **recreational watercraft** depending on jurisdiction. It's more regulated than land-based devices because water use involves additional safety concerns.

### Federal Classification (United States)

**1. US Coast Guard Classification**
- The USCG classifies vessels by size and power
- Our hovercraft: <5m length, <10HP equivalent (800W lift + 500W thrust = 1.7HP total)
- Falls under **"recreational vessel"** — minimal federal requirements
- No federal registration required for vessels under 5m with <10HP

**2. State-Level Registration**
- Some states require registration for all motorized watercraft
- Others exempt vessels under a certain size or power
- Check your state's specific requirements

**3. EPA Regulations**
- Zero emissions — no EPA regulations apply
- No engine emissions testing required

## Why It's Legal

### 1. Falls Within Recreational Watercraft Category
- Personal watercraft (Jet Skis, etc.) are legal in all 50 states
- Our hovercraft is smaller and slower than most PWCs
- The phi-harmonic design is an internal feature — no different from choosing a specific hull design

### 2. Low Power
- Total power: 1300W (1.7HP equivalent)
- Most states regulate vessels by horsepower
- Under 10HP: minimal regulation
- Under 5HP: often exempt from registration

### 3. Zero Emissions
- No EPA regulations apply
- No fuel storage or spillage risk
- No exhaust emissions

### 4. Small Size
- Platform: 1200mm × 800mm (4ft × 2.6ft)
- Under 5m length: federal exemption
- Under 14-16ft: state exemption in most states

## How to Stay Legal

### Do's
- Check state registration requirements before first use
- Register if required (usually $20-50/year)
- Follow all local waterway rules
- Wear life jacket (required in most states for PWC)
- Operate at safe speeds near shore
- Yield to larger vessels
- Use navigation lights if operating at dusk/night

### Don'ts
- Don't operate near swimming areas
- Don't operate near boat ramps or marinas
- Don't operate in shipping channels
- Don't operate under the influence of alcohol or drugs
- Don't operate at night without proper lighting
- Don't carry passengers (single-rider design)

## Insurance Considerations

- **Boat insurance**: May be required by some states
- **Homeowner's insurance**: May cover watercraft liability
- **Umbrella policy**: Recommended for additional coverage
- **Cost**: Typically $100-300/year for small watercraft
