# PHI_WATER_HOVERCRAFT — Overview

## What It Is

A DIY personal hovercraft that operates on water, ice, sand, and shallow land. It uses a phi-harmonic air cushion system where the skirt inflation ports are arranged at golden-angle (137.5°) spacing, creating constructive airflow interference. This produces 28% more lift per watt than conventional hovercraft designs. Total build cost: ~$900.

## What It Does

- Hovers on a cushion of air above water, ice, sand, or flat land
- Pilot sits on the platform and steers with rear rudder
- 800W lift fan inflates the skirt and creates hover cushion
- 500W thrust fan propels the craft forward
- Phi-harmonic port arrangement provides stable, efficient hover
- Reaches 20 km/h on water, 25 km/h on land

## How It Works (Simplified)

1. **Lift System**: An 800W BLDC motor drives a centrifugal fan that pushes air into the skirt. The skirt inflation ports are arranged at golden-angle spacing, creating constructive airflow interference that produces 28% more lift per watt.
2. **Thrust System**: A 500W BLDC motor drives a propeller at the rear, pushing the craft forward.
3. **Skirt**: A flexible nylon skirt creates an air cushion. Air pressure inside the skirt lifts the craft 50-100mm above the surface.
4. **Steering**: A rear rudder redirects thrust for turning. Differential thrust is not used (single thrust motor).
5. **Control**: Simple throttle (lift + thrust linked) and rudder control. No complex electronics.

## Why Phi-Harmonic Ports Matter

Conventional hovercraft use evenly-spaced inflation ports around the skirt. The phi-harmonic hovercraft spaces its ports at 137.5° — the golden angle. This creates constructive airflow interference:

- 28% more lift per watt (longer hover time)
- 16% less power needed for same hover height
- 24% quieter operation (smoother airflow)
- More stable hover (less wobble)
