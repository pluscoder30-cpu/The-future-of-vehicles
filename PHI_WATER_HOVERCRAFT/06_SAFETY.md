# PHI_WATER_HOVERCRAFT — Safety Precautions

## CRITICAL SAFETY WARNINGS

> **READ ALL WARNINGS BEFORE OPERATING THE HOVERCRAFT**
> **FAILURE TO FOLLOW SAFETY RULES CAN RESULT IN SERIOUS INJURY OR DEATH**

---

## Water Safety (PRIMARY HAZARD)

### Drowning Risk
- **ALWAYS wear a USCG-approved life jacket** when on water
- Never operate alone — have a spotter on shore
- Stay in shallow water (knee-deep) until experienced
- Never operate in rough water, strong currents, or high winds
- Know how to swim before operating on water
- Keep whistle accessible for emergency signaling

### Water Conditions
- **Only operate on calm, still water** (lakes, ponds, calm bays)
- Never operate in rivers, oceans, or flowing water
- Avoid water with debris, vegetation, or obstacles
- Check weather forecast before each use
- Stop operation if wind exceeds 15 km/h
- Never operate in thunderstorms

### Cold Water
- Water below 15°C (60°F) requires wetsuit or drysuit
- Cold water causes hypothermia in minutes
- Know the signs: shivering, confusion, loss of coordination
- Have warm, dry clothes available after each use

---

## Electrical Safety

### Battery Hazards
- **LiFePO4 batteries store significant energy** — short circuit can cause fire
- Never expose battery to temperatures above 60°C (140°F)
- Never puncture, crush, or disassemble battery cells
- If battery swells, smells, or smokes — **STOP USE IMMEDIATELY**
- Keep battery enclosure sealed and waterproof
- **Charge only with the supplied 48V LiFePO4 charger**
- Never charge battery near water or in wet conditions

### High Voltage
- The battery operates at **48V DC** — this can cause burns
- Never touch battery terminals with bare hands
- Always disconnect battery before working on electronics
- Use insulated tools when working near live circuits
- Keep all electrical connections waterproofed

---

## Mechanical Safety

### Propeller Hazards
- **NEVER touch the propeller while motor is running**
- Install propeller guard — never operate without it
- Keep all body parts and clothing away from propeller
- Stop motor completely before approaching propeller
- Check propeller for damage before each use

### Fan Hazards
- Keep hands and loose clothing away from lift fan
- Never operate with fan shroud removed
- Check fan for damage before each use
- Stop motor completely before approaching fan

### Rudder
- Keep feet and legs clear of rudder mechanism
- Do not force rudder beyond its range of motion
- Check rudder cables for wear before each use

## Emergency Procedures

### Craft Malfunction on Water
1. **Release throttle immediately**
2. Stop both motors
3. If craft is drifting, use paddle or hands to return to shore
4. If craft is sinking, abandon and swim to shore (with life jacket)
5. Signal for help if needed

### Battery Emergency
1. If battery smokes or catches fire: **DO NOT USE WATER** (electrical fire)
2. Move away from the craft
3. If on water, swim away from the craft
4. Use Class D fire extinguisher (metal fire) if available
5. Call emergency services (911)

### Propeller/Fan Injury
1. Stop all motors immediately
2. Apply first aid to wound
3. Call emergency services if bleeding is severe
4. Do not remove embedded objects — seek medical help

### Capsizing
1. Stay with the craft (it provides flotation)
2. Keep life jacket on
3. Signal for help
4. Swim to shore only if safe to do so
