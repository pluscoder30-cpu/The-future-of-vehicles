# PHI_CARGO_CART — Regulatory Status

## Legal Classification

The phi-harmonic cargo cart is classified as a **personal electric utility device** or **electric cart**. It's largely unregulated because it falls within existing categories for personal-use electric carts.

### Federal Classification (United States)

**1. Not a "Motor Vehicle"**
- Under 49 USC §30102, a "motor vehicle" requires an internal combustion engine or is designed for use on public streets
- The cart uses an electric motor and is designed for personal utility use
- No EPA emissions standards apply (zero emissions)
- No NHTSA standards apply (not designed for public roads)

**2. Consumer Product Safety**
- The CPSC regulates consumer products including electric carts
- Battery should meet UL 2271 standard (LiFePO4)
- Motor should meet IEC 60335-1 standard

**3. Not a "Motorized Wheelchair"**
- The ADA defines motorized wheelchairs as assistive devices
- The cart is a utility device, not an assistive device
- ADA requirements don't apply

## Why It Bypasses Vehicle Laws

### 1. Personal Use Only
- Not designed for commercial transport
- Not designed for public road use
- Personal utility device classification
- Similar to shopping carts, garden carts

### 2. Low Speed
- Maximum speed: 20 km/h (12.4 mph)
- Most states regulate personal mobility devices by speed:
  - Under 15 mph: Unregulated
  - 15-25 mph: Light regulation
  - Over 25 mph: Full regulation
- Our cart is at the light regulation threshold

### 3. Zero Emissions
- No EPA regulations apply
- No fuel storage or spillage risk
- No exhaust emissions

### 4. Small Size
- Cart: 800mm × 600mm (2.6ft × 2ft)
- Under most size thresholds for regulation
- Similar to shopping cart dimensions

### 5. Pedestrian-Compatible
- Operates on sidewalks at pedestrian speeds
- Yields to pedestrians
- No different from electric shopping carts used in stores

## How to Stay Legal

### Do's
- Follow local sidewalk rules (yield to pedestrians)
- Keep speed below local limits (typically 10-15 mph)
- Use bike lanes where available
- Check city ordinances before operating in new areas
- Use lights at night (recommended)
- Register as a "personal utility device" if your city requires it

### Don'ts
- Don't exceed local speed limits
- Don't operate in traffic lanes (unless no sidewalk available)
- Don't operate under the influence of alcohol or drugs
- Don't block pedestrian pathways
- Don't carry passengers (single-operator design)
- Don't use commercially (deliveries) without permits
