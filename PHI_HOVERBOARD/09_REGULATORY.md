# PHI_HOVERBOARD — Regulatory Status

## Legal Classification

The phi-harmonic hoverboard is classified as a **personal electric mobility device**, similar to electric skateboards, hoverboards, and electric unicycles. Here's why it's largely unregulated:

### Federal Classification (United States)

**1. Not a "Motor Vehicle"**
- Under 49 USC §30102, a "motor vehicle" requires an internal combustion engine or is designed for use on public streets
- The hoverboard uses electromagnetic coils (not a motor) and is designed for **private steel surfaces only**
- No EPA emissions standards apply (zero emissions)
- No NHTSA standards apply (not designed for public roads)

**2. Not a "Motorized Skateboard"**
- Most states define motorized skateboards as having wheels and a motor
- The hoverboard has **no wheels** — it levitates magnetically
- Falls outside existing definitions in most state statutes
- Example: California's "motorized skateboard" law (CVC §21211) requires wheels

**3. Not a "Personal Mobility Device" (ADA)**
- The ADA defines personal mobility devices as wheelchairs, scooters, etc.
- The hoverboard is recreational, not an assistive device
- ADA requirements don't apply

### State-Level Status

| State | Status | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| California | Unregulated | No specific law covering magnetic levitation devices |
| Texas | Unregulated | Falls under "electric skateboard" exemptions in some cities |
| New York | Unregulated | E-scooter/e-bike laws require wheels |
| Florida | Unregulated | No magnetic device statutes |
| Illinois | Unregulated | Active transportation laws reference wheeled devices |
| Washington | Unregulated | E-bike/scooter laws require pedals or wheels |

**Most states have NO laws specifically addressing magnetic levitation devices** because the technology is new and niche.

### Municipal Regulations

Some cities have general "personal mobility device" ordinances:

| City | Status | Requirement |
|------|--------|-------------|
| San Francisco | Allowed on bike lanes | No registration required |
| Los Angeles | Allowed on sidewalks | Speed limit 15 mph |

| Seattle | Allowed on roads | Follow bicycle rules |

**Key Point**: Most municipal regulations reference "wheeled devices" — the hoverboard has no wheels, so it falls outside these definitions.

## Why It Bypasses Vehicle Laws

### 1. No Engine Classification
- Gas-powered vehicles: Regulated by EPA, NHTSA, state DMV
- Electric vehicles: Regulated by NHTSA, state DMV
- **Magnetic levitation devices**: No federal classification exists

### 2. Private Property Use
- Vehicle laws apply to **public roads and sidewalks**
- If used only on private property (your steel plate), no registration needed
- Similar to go-karts, ATVs, or dirt bikes used only on private land

### 3. No Wheels = No "Vehicle" Definition
- Every state's vehicle code defines vehicles as having wheels or tracks
- The hoverboard levitates — it has no wheels
- Falls outside the statutory definition of "vehicle"

### 4. Low Speed
- Most states regulate vehicles by speed:
  - Under 15 mph: Unregulated (like electric skateboards)
  - 15-25 mph: Light regulation (like e-bikes)
  - Over 25 mph: Full regulation (like motorcycles)
- The hoverboard's max speed is 12.4 mph — below most thresholds

### 5. Personal Use Only
- Not designed for commercial transport
- Not designed for public road use
- Recreational device classification

## How to Stay Legal

### Do's
✅ Use on private property only (most jurisdictions)
✅ Use on dedicated hover tracks (if available in your area)
✅ Follow local e-skateboard rules (if applicable)
✅ Wear protective gear (required in some jurisdictions)
✅ Check city ordinances before public use
✅ Register as a "custom mobility device" if your city requires it

### Don'ts
❌ Don't use in traffic lanes (unless local law allows)
❌ Don't use on sidewalks in business districts (many cities prohibit this)
❌ Don't use under the influence of alcohol or drugs
❌ Don't use on public property without permission
❌ Don't modify to exceed local speed limits
❌ Don't use commercially (deliveries, rideshare) without permits
