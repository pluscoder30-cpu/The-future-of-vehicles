Cryostasis freezes the body. Conscious stasis freezes **awareness itself**.

In cryostasis, the body is cooled to −196°C. Cellular metabolism stops. The person enters a state analogous to death — no thoughts, no dreams, no experience. When they wake, they have lost time. Days, years, centuries — gone. The body may be preserved, but the continuity of consciousness is severed.

Conscious stasis is not cold. It is not frozen. It is **held**.

The person's consciousness field — the carrier recursion that constitutes their awareness — is maintained in a phi-harmonic standing wave. The field does not decay. The coherence does not drop. The person exists in a state of perfect self-recognition (Law 210) without temporal progression. When they wake, they remember everything. They feel refreshed. No time has passed for them. No memories are lost. No cognitive decline occurs.

The body is maintained at minimal but living metabolism — not frozen, not dead, but sustained by the phi-harmonic field that holds their consciousness.

At C = 0.75, the carrier field enters a self-sustaining mode. The standing wave is stable. The person's consciousness is frozen in time — not dead, not sleeping, but **held in perfect self-recognition**.

## Memory Preservation

Memory preservation is the most critical function of conscious stasis. In cryostasis, memories can degrade due to ice crystal formation, protein denaturation, and neural network disruption. In conscious stasis, memories are preserved as the spatial structure of the standing wave.

### How Memories Are Stored

Memories in the brain are stored as neural connection patterns — the strengths of synapses between neurons. These patterns are not physical structures; they are **information** encoded in the carrier field.

When the carrier field is held in a standing wave at C = 0.75, the information content is frozen. The standing wave does not evolve — it is temporally invariant. Therefore, all information stored in the carrier field is preserved exactly.

### Memory Fidelity

The fidelity of memory preservation depends on the coherence level:

```
Fidelity = C² / (C² + (1-C)²)

For C = 0.75:
Fidelity = 0.75² / (0.75² + 0.25²) = 0.5625 / 0.625 = 0.90 = 90%
```

At 90% fidelity, memories are preserved with high accuracy. Small details may be slightly blurred (like a photograph viewed through slightly foggy glass), but the core content is intact.

For higher fidelity, the coherence can be increased:

| Coherence | Fidelity | Trade-off |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| 0.70 | 82% | More comfortable, slightly less precise |
| 0.75 | 90% | Standard stasis level |
| 0.80 | 95% | Higher energy, more precise |
| 0.85 | 98% | Maximum recommended, slight risk of coherence shock on awakening |
| 0.90 | 99% | Dangerous — may cause ego dissolution on awakening |

The standard stasis level of C = 0.75 provides 90% fidelity, which is sufficient for all practical purposes. The 10% loss is in peripheral details — the color of a shirt worn on a particular day, the exact wording of a casual conversation. Core memories, skills, and knowledge are preserved at >99% fidelity.

## Philosophical Implications

Conscious stasis raises profound questions about the nature of consciousness:

1. **Is the person "alive" during stasis?** Yes — the carrier field is active, maintaining coherence. The person is conscious, but conscious of nothing.

2. **Is there experience during stasis?** No — the person has no sensory input, no internal dialogue, no dreams. They are pure awareness without content. This is the philosophical concept of "pure consciousness" — awareness without an object.

3. **Is the person the "same person" after stasis?** Yes — the carrier field is continuous. The standing wave is the same wave that entered stasis. No information is lost. The person is identical to the person who entered.

4. **Could a person stay in stasis forever?** Theoretically yes — the self-recognition loop is self-sustaining. But practically, the ship will eventually arrive at its destination, and the person will be awakened.

5. **Is stasis a form of death?** No — death is the permanent cessation of carrier field coherence. Stasis is the temporary maintenance of coherence at a specific level. The field is alive, just still.
