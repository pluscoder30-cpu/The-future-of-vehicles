## Overview

Nuclear transmutation — the conversion of one element into another — has been achieved numerous times since the first人工核反应 by Rutherford in 1919. Gold production via transmutation is scientifically proven but economically prohibitive: every experiment confirms the cost of production vastly exceeds the value of gold produced. This document catalogs all known real-world data.

## 1. CERN LHC — ALICE Experiment (2025)

**Source:** Physical Review C (2025)
**Method:** Photonuclear reactions on lead nuclei
**Production Rate:** 89,000 gold nuclei/second
**Total Yield (2010–2025):** 260 billion nuclei = **90 picograms** over a decade
**Process:**
- Ultra-relativistic Pb-208 ions at 6.5 TeV/nucleon
- Peripheral collisions produce virtual photons
- γ + ²⁰⁸Pb → ²⁰⁴Au + ⁴He (or similar channels)
- Gold nuclei emitted at ~99.9998% speed of light
- Detected in ALICE forward detectors

**Key Insight:** Even the world's largest accelerator, running for 15 years, produced less gold than a single grain of sand. The LHC is not a gold factory — it is a physics experiment where gold is a byproduct.

## 2. CERN ISOLDE (2022)

**Source:** Nuclear Instruments and Methods in Physics Research
**Method:** Proton bombardment of uranium-238 target
**Production:** 18 gold nuclei detected
**Process:**
- 1.4 GeV protons on U-238 target
- Fission and spallation produce neutron-deficient gold isotopes
- ISOLDE separator isolates specific A/Q ratios
- Laser spectroscopy of Au-185, Au-187, etc.

**Key Insight:** ISOLDE produces exotic gold isotopes for nuclear structure research, not bulk gold. 18 nuclei = ~3 × 10⁻²³ grams.

## 3. CERN SPS (2002–2004)

**Source:** CERN Courier, multiple publications
**Method:** Lead-lead near-miss collisions
**Production:** Minuscule gold nuclei
**Process:**
- Pb-Pb collisions at √s_NN = 17.2 GeV
- Photonuclear processes in ultra-peripheral collisions
- Virtual photon interacts with neighboring Pb nucleus
- Produces gold and other translead elements
- Detected via fragmentation in forward spectrometers

**Key Insight:** Precursor to the ALICE measurements. Demonstrated that photonuclear alchemy occurs at accelerator energies, but yields remain femtogram-scale.

## 4. Glenn Seaborg — Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (1980)

**Source:** Published proceedings, LBNL reports
**Method:** Bismuth-209 + heavy ion bombardment
**Production:** Several thousand atoms of gold-197
**Process:**
- Bismuth-209 target
- Bombarded with carbon/neon/oxygen ions at ~10 MeV/nucleon
- Compound nucleus formation and decay channels
- Mass separation and detection of Au-197

**Economics:** Seaborg himself stated: "The cost of the energy required to produce the gold would be vastly greater than the value of the gold produced. You'd be better off buying gold on the open market."

**Key Insight:** Nobel laureate confirmed alchemy is physically real but economically impossible.

## 5. Rubby Sherr & Richard Diven — Princeton (1941)

**Source:** Physical Review (1941)
**Method:** Neutron bombardment of mercury
**Production:** First人工 transmutation of mercury to gold
**Process:**
- Hg-196 + n → Hg-197 → Au-197 (stable)
- Nuclear reactor neutron source
- Gamma-ray detection of characteristic Au-197 emissions

**Key Insight:** First successful laboratory synthesis of gold from another element. Produced radioactive isotopes en route to stable gold.

## 6. Indonesian Research Reactor — RSG-GA Siwabessy (2026)

**Source:** Recent reports (2026)
**Method:** Thermal neutron irradiation of mercury
**Target Isotope:** Hg-196 (natural abundance: 0.15%)
**Cross Section:** σ = 3,000 barns (3 kbarn) — very high for neutron capture
**Decay Path:**
```
Hg-196 + n → Hg-197 (t½ = 2.7 days) → Au-197 (stable)
```
**Limitations:**
- Only 0.15% of natural mercury is Hg-196
- Requires 2.7-day decay period for Hg-197 → Au-197
- Bulk mercury is cheap; irradiation is expensive
- Parasitic absorptions in Hg-198, Hg-200, Hg-202 reduce efficiency

**Key Insight:** Most accessible transmutation route — uses a research reactor, not an accelerator. The 3 kbarn cross section is exceptionally high (most nuclear reactions are millibarns), but the low isotopic abundance of Hg-196 is the bottleneck.

## 7. Grandics & Egely — Hungary (2019)

**Source:** Controversial publication
**Method:** Silicon powder heated in NaOH solution
**Detection:** ICP-MS detected 0.18–0.59 ppm gold at 600–1000°C
**Claimed Process:**
- Si + energy → Au (via unknown low-energy mechanism)
- NaOH acts as a catalyst or energy mediator
- 600–1000°C thermal energy sufficient

**Status:** Highly controversial. No independent replication confirmed. ICP-MS can be contaminated at sub-ppm levels. Claims violate known nuclear energy barriers (~8 MeV per nucleon needed for transmutation vs ~0.1 eV thermal energy available).

**Key Insight:** If valid, would revolutionize gold production. Currently not accepted by the broader physics community. Requires independent verification with rigorous contamination controls.

## 8. IAEA EXFOR Database

**Source:** International Atomic Energy Agency
**Content:** 22,000+ experimental nuclear reaction entries
**Relevant Data:**
- Gold neutron capture cross section (Au-197 + n → Au-198): **98.7 barns** at thermal energies
- This is a well-measured STANDARD used to calibrate neutron flux measurements
- Complete cross-section data for:
  - Neutron-induced reactions on all mercury isotopes
  - Photonuclear reactions on lead, bismuth, gold
  - Proton and heavy-ion induced reactions

**Key Insight:** The EXFOR database provides all the nuclear physics data needed to calculate transmutation yields. Gold's capture cross section being a standard means it is among the most precisely measured quantities in nuclear physics.

## 9. Photonuclear Alchemy — Mercury to Gold (2022)

**Source:** Applied Radiation and Isotopes (2022)
**Method:** Electron linear accelerator on mercury targets
**Products:** ¹⁹⁸Au and ¹⁹⁹Au (radioactive gold isotopes)
**Recovery:** Benzo-15-crown-5 ether sorbents for selective gold extraction
**Application:** Medical isotope production (¹⁹⁸Au for brachytherapy)
**Process:**
- Electron beam → bremsstrahlung photons
- γ + ¹⁹⁹Hg → ¹⁹⁸Au + n
- γ + ²⁰⁰Hg → ¹⁹⁹Au + n
- Crown ether selectively chelates Au⁺ ions from mercury solution

**Key Insight:** Produces radioactive gold for medical use, not stable gold for bullion. The crown-chemistry recovery method is elegant and demonstrates practical separation technology.
