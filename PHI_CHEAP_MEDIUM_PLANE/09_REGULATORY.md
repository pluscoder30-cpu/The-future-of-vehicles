# PHI_CHEAP_MEDIUM_PLANE — Regulatory Compliance

## 1. FAA CLASSIFICATION

### 1.1 Category Determination
The PHI_CHEAP_MEDIUM_PLANE at 800 kg empty weight exceeds Part 103 limits:
- Part 103 max empty weight: 317 kg (70 lb)
- This aircraft: 800 kg (1764 lb)
- Classification: **Experimental Amateur-Built** (FAR Part 21.191)

### 1.2 Applicable Regulations
| Regulation | Description | Status |
|---|---|---|
| FAR Part 21.191 | Experimental amateur-built | Primary category |
| FAR Part 21.183 | Airworthiness certificate | Required |
| FAR Part 43 | Maintenance | Required |
| FAR Part 91 | General operating rules | Applicable |
| FAR Part 103 | Ultralight vehicles | NOT applicable (weight) |
| ASTM F2245 | Light sport aircraft | Reference only |
| EAA guidelines | Homebuilt aircraft | Reference |

### 1.3 Experimental Amateur-Built Requirements
Under FAR Part 21.191(g):
- Must be fabricated and assembled by the applicant (51% rule)
- Must not be a product of a kit (unless meeting 51% rule)
- Operating limitations issued by local FSDO
- Initial airworthiness certificate issued after inspection
- Annual condition inspection required

## 2. 51% RULE COMPLIANCE

### 2.1 What Counts as Homebuilt
The aircraft must be "major portion" (51%) fabricated by the builder:

**Allowed to have others build** (49% max):
- Pre-fabricated components from commercial sources
- Standard hardware (bolts, nuts, rivets)
- Commercial avionics and instruments
- Used aircraft parts (landing gear, wheels, brakes)
- Motors and ESCs (commercial)
- Battery cells (commercial)

**Must build yourself** (51% min):
- Fuselage frame (aluminum tube cutting, bending, assembly)
- Wing structure (spar cutting, rib fabrication, assembly)
- Empennage (fabrication and assembly)
- Control system (fabrication and rigging)
- Electrical wiring (cutting, terminating, routing)
- Battery box fabrication
- Instrument panel fabrication
- Seat fabrication
- Fairings and non-structural components

### 2.2 Builder's Log Requirements
Maintain a detailed builder's log documenting:
- All fabrication steps with dates
- Materials used and sources
- Inspection points and results
- Photographs of construction stages
- Time spent on each phase (minimum 500 hours)

## 3. AIRWORTHINESS CERTIFICATE

### 3.1 Application Process
1. Complete construction (51% builder work)
2. Contact local FSDO (Flight Standards District Office)
3. Schedule inspection with Designated Airworthiness Representative (DAR) or FSDO inspector
4. Present aircraft for inspection
5. Provide builder's log documentation
6. Receive operating limitations
7. Receive airworthiness certificate (experimental category)

### 3.2 Operating Limitations
Standard limitations for experimental amateur-built:
- Cannot carry passengers or property for compensation or hire
- Cannot operate over densely populated areas
- Cannot operate in controlled airspace without ATC authorization
- Day VFR only (unless equipped for night VFR)
- Within 300 nm of builder's base (unless ferry permit)
- Must display "EXPERIMENTAL" placard
- Passenger limitation: per operating limitations (usually pilot + passengers)
- Maintenance: owner-performed or A&P supervision

### 3.3 Phase I Flight Testing
Required flight testing period:
- Minimum 40 hours flight testing
- Within 25 nm of home base
- No passengers during Phase I
- Pilot must hold private pilot certificate minimum
- Test flights logged in builder's log
- Report any discrepancies to FSDO

## 4. ELECTRIC AIRCRAFT SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS

### 4.1 Battery Safety
- All batteries must be UL listed or equivalent
- BMS required for each battery group
- Battery boxes must be fire-resistant
- Ventilation required in battery compartment
- Battery disconnect required (master switch)
- Emergency battery disconnect required

### 4.2 Electrical System
- All wiring must be aviation-grade (MIL-SPEC or equivalent)
- Circuit protection required on all circuits
- Ground fault protection recommended
- EMC compliance for all avionics
- Shielding required for engine control wiring

### 4.3 Motor Requirements
- Motors must be rated for aviation use (or equivalent)
- Temperature monitoring required
- Redundant motor systems recommended (twin-engine)
- Motor mount must meet structural requirements
- Propeller must be balanced and inspected

---

## 5. INSPECTION REQUIREMENTS

### 5.1 Annual Condition Inspection
Required annually:
- Structural inspection (frame, wings, empennage)
- Landing gear inspection
- Control system inspection
- Electrical system inspection
- Battery condition inspection
- Motor inspection
- Propeller inspection
- Avionics functional check
- ELT inspection and test
- Weight and balance verification

### 5.2 Inspection Authority
Who can perform the annual inspection:
- A&P mechanic (Inspection Authorization preferred)
- Pilot (owner, for minor items only)
- DAR (Designated Airworthiness Representative)
- EAA Technical Counselor

### 5.3 Inspection Documentation
Maintain records of:
- Date of inspection
- Inspector name and certificate number
- Items inspected
- Discrepancies found
- Corrective actions taken
- Return to service endorsement

---

## 6. PILOT REQUIREMENTS

### 6.1 Minimum Pilot Certificate
- Private Pilot Certificate (minimum)
- Airplane single-engine land rating (or multi-engine if applicable)
- Current medical certificate (3rd class minimum)
- Current flight review

### 6.2 Additional Training (Recommended)
- Transition training specific to this aircraft type
- Emergency procedures training
- Single-engine operations training (twin-engine)
- Electric aircraft systems familiarization
- Minimum 10 hours dual instruction in type

### 6.3 Currency Requirements
- 3 takeoffs and landings within preceding 90 days (day)
- 3 takeoffs and landings within preceding 90 days (night, if night rated)
- Current flight review (every 24 calendar months)
- Medical certificate current

## 9. MODIFICATION AND REPAIR

### 9.1 Minor Modifications
Minor modifications (non-structural, non-flight critical):
- Builder can perform
- Document in builder's log
- Include in annual inspection

### 9.2 Major Modifications
Major modifications (structural, flight critical):
- Must be performed by A&P or with A&P supervision
- Requires engineering data (stress analysis)
- May require FSDO approval
- Update weight and balance
- Update aircraft records

### 9.3 Repairs
- Minor repairs: A&P can perform and return to service
- Major repairs: A&P can perform, IA must inspect
- Structural repairs: require engineering data
- Electrical repairs: must meet original standards

---

## 10. OPERATIONAL RESTRICTIONS

### 10.1 Standard Experimental Limitations
1. No flight over densely populated areas
2. No flight for compensation or hire
3. No flight in controlled airspace without ATC authorization
4. Day VFR only (unless specifically approved for night)
5. Within 300 nm of base (unless ferry permit obtained)
6. No passengers during Phase I testing
7. Must display "EXPERIMENTAL" placard prominently

### 10.2 Electric-Specific Considerations
1. Monitor battery state of charge continuously
2. Plan flights with 30% energy reserve minimum
3. Do not operate in icing conditions
4. Do not charge batteries unattended
5. Do not operate with battery temperature above 60C
6. Emergency procedures posted in cockpit
7. Fire extinguisher required (Halotron recommended)

### 10.3 International Operations
- Obtain radio station license before international flight
- Comply with destination country regulations
- May need import/export documentation
- Insurance coverage must extend to destination
- Contact destination country aviation authority in advance
