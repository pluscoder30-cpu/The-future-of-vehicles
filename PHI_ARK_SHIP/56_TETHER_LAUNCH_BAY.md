# 56 — TETHER LAUNCH BAY

## Overview

The Tether Launch Bay is a phi-harmonic tether system for deploying and retrieving small craft — drones, vehicles, probes, and emergency pods — using energy-efficient electromagnetic tethers instead of chemical propulsion. The system uses 10 launch bays distributed across the ship's hull, each capable of launching or retrieving craft at velocities up to 10 km/s without expelling any propellant. The tether is a superconducting cable that stores and releases kinetic energy through electromagnetic interaction with the ship's magnetic field.

**Design Philosophy**: Traditional launch systems expel mass (propellant) to accelerate craft. The Tether Launch Bay expels no mass — it uses a superconducting tether to transfer kinetic energy between the ship and the craft. The ship's immense mass provides an essentially infinite momentum reservoir. A 10,000 kg craft can be accelerated to 10 km/s while the ship's velocity changes by 0.000000001 m/s — undetectable.

## The Physics of Electromagnetic Tethers

### Tether Mechanics

The electromagnetic tether works on the principle of Lorentz force interaction between a current-carrying conductor and a magnetic field:

```
F = I × L × B

Where:
  F = Force on tether (Newtons)
  I = Current in tether (Amperes)
  L = Length of tether (meters)
  B = Magnetic field strength (Tesla)
```

The tether is a superconducting cable that carries current through the ship's magnetic field, generating a force that accelerates the attached craft. By controlling the current, the force can be precisely varied — from φ-ground (α_min) to maximum thrust.

**Tether force calculation**:

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Tether length | 5,000 m (deployed) |
| Current | 10,000 A |
| Magnetic field (ship's field at hull) | 0.1 T |
| Force | 50 MN (50,000,000 N) |
| Craft mass | 10,000 kg |
| Acceleration | 5,000 m/s² (500 g) |
| Launch velocity (5,000 m tether) | 10,000 m/s (10 km/s) |
| Launch time | 2 seconds |

### Energy Storage and Recovery

The tether system stores energy kinetically — the craft itself becomes the energy storage medium. During launch, the ship's rotational energy (or stored flywheel energy) is transferred to the craft via the tether. During retrieval, the craft's kinetic energy is transferred back to the ship.

**Energy balance**:

| Phase | Energy Source | Energy Destination | Efficiency |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|------------|
| Launch | Ship kinetic energy | Craft kinetic energy | 95% |
| Retrieval | Craft kinetic energy | Ship kinetic energy | 90% |
| Net loss per cycle | — | Heat (tether resistance) | 5% |
| Net energy per cycle | — | — | ~5% loss |

The 5% energy loss per cycle is negligible — it's equivalent to the heat generated by a few light bulbs relative to the ship's total energy.

**Deployment sequence**:

| Step | Action | Time | Distance |
|------|--------|------|----------|
