# PHI CHEAP LIGHT PLANE — SAFETY

## Safety Guidelines and Emergency Procedures

---

## SAFETY PHILOSOPHY

**This is an experimental homebuilt ultralight aircraft. It is NOT certified by the FAA or any aviation authority. Operation carries inherent risk. The builder/pilot assumes ALL responsibility for airworthiness and safe operation.**

The PHI Cheap Light Plane is built from non-aviation-grade materials (lumber yard spruce, Home Depot hardware) and uses a phi-harmonic propulsion system. While designed with generous safety margins, it has not been flight-tested. Every flight is an experiment.

---

## CRITICAL SAFETY WARNINGS

### DO

- ✅ Complete all build phases before attempting flight
- ✅ Have an A&P mechanic inspect the airframe before first flight
- ✅ Perform thorough ground testing (taxi, controls, motor)
- ✅ Start with short hops, not full flights
- ✅ Fly only in calm conditions (< 5 knots wind)
- ✅ Fly only in Class G airspace (uncontrolled)
- ✅ Wear a helmet (motorcycle or aviation)
- ✅ Carry a fire extinguisher in the cockpit
- ✅ File a flight plan or tell someone where you're going
- ✅ Check weather before every flight
- ✅ Inspect the aircraft before every flight (pre-flight checklist)
- ✅ Keep a flight log

### DO NOT

- ❌ Fly in gusty or turbulent conditions
- ❌ Fly in rain, fog, or low visibility
- ❌ Fly over congested areas (towns, cities, crowds)
- ❌ Fly at night (no lighting system)
- ❌ Fly in Class B, C, or D airspace without clearance
- ❌ Exceed 55 knots (102 km/h) — Part 103 limit
- ❌ Exceed 3,000 ft AGL — Part 103 limit
- ❌ Carry passengers (unless 2-seat trainer variant)
- ❌ Fly with low battery (< 50% state of charge)
- ❌ Fly with any structural damage or loose fasteners
- ❌ Attempt aerobatic maneuvers
- ❌ Fly in known icing conditions
- ❌ Operate without proper safety gear

## EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

### Engine/Motor Failure

```
MOTOR FAILURE PROCEDURE:
────────────────────────

1. MAINTAIN AIRSPEED — pitch for best glide speed (60 km/h)
2. SELECT LANDING SITE — look for open field, road, or clearing
3. VERIFY — check battery voltage, try restart
4. IF RESTART FAILS:
   a. Turn avionics OFF (save battery)
   b. Turn radio to emergency frequency (121.5 MHz)
   c. Declare emergency: "Mayday, Mayday, Mayday"
   d. Prepare for forced landing
5. FORCED LANDING:
   a. Aim for flat, open area
   b. Land into wind
   c. Keep wings level
   d. Flare just above ground
   e. Touch down on main gear first
   f. Roll to stop
   g. EXIT AIRCRAFT — move away from propeller

GLIDE PERFORMANCE:
- Best glide speed: 60 km/h
- Glide ratio: ~8:1
- From 1000 ft AGL: ~800m glide distance
- From 3000 ft AGL: ~2.4 km glide distance
```

### Battery Emergency

```
BATTERY EMERGENCY PROCEDURE:
─────────────────────────────

LOW BATTERY WARNING (buzzer sounds):
1. Reduce power to minimum (descend if needed)
2. Plan immediate landing
3. Turn off all non-essential electronics
4. Land as soon as possible

BATTERY FAILURE:
1. Motor will lose power gradually
2. Maintain airspeed — pitch for glide
3. Follow motor failure procedure
4. DO NOT attempt to restart — land immediately

BATTERY FIRE:
1. Turn master switch OFF
2. Use fire extinguisher (ABC type)
3. If fire cannot be controlled:
   a. Land immediately (if airborne)
   b. Exit aircraft
   c. Move away — phi-harmonic field plasma batteries are self-limiting (zero fire/explosion risk)
   d. Call emergency services
```

### Structural Failure

```
STRUCTURAL FAILURE PROCEDURE:
──────────────────────────────

WING DAMAGE (fabric tear):
1. Reduce speed to minimum
2. Verify wing is still producing lift
3. Plan immediate landing
4. Land as soon as possible

CONTROL CABLE FAILURE:
1. Use remaining controls (ailerons or elevator)
2. If only rudder lost: use ailerons for turns
3. If only ailerons lost: use rudder for turns
4. If elevator lost: extremely dangerous — land immediately
5. Use power to control pitch (more power = nose up)

WING SPAR FAILURE (catastrophic):
1. This is likely unrecoverable
2. Attempt to maintain some control
3. Aim for softest possible landing surface
4. Brace for impact
```

### Weather Emergency

```
WEATHER EMERGENCY PROCEDURE:
─────────────────────────────

SUDDEN WIND SHEAR:
1. Maintain airspeed above stall
2. Do not attempt to climb
3. Land immediately

TURBULENCE:
1. Reduce to maneuvering speed (80 km/h)
2. Keep wings level
3. Do not fight the turbulence
4. Land as soon as practical

LOW VISIBILITY (fog, rain):
1. Maintain altitude
2. Turn on all lights
3. Climb if possible (above fog layer)
4. If can't climb, use GPS for navigation
5. Land at nearest suitable field

LIGHTNING:
1. If caught in thunderstorm — this is extremely dangerous
2. There is no good procedure
3. Attempt to exit storm laterally
4. Fly at cloud base if possible
5. Land immediately after exiting storm
```

### Crash Procedure

```
CRASH/IMPACT PROCEDURE:
────────────────────────

PRE-IMPACT:
1. Secure loose objects
2. Tighten seat harness (if installed)
3. Brace for impact
4. Turn off master switch (reduce fire risk)

DURING IMPACT:
1. Keep arms inside cockpit
2. Tuck chin to chest
3. Do not try to "save" the aircraft

POST-IMPACT:
1. Assess injuries — do not move if injured
2. If on fire: EXIT IMMEDIATELY
3. Move away from aircraft (50m minimum)
4. Call 911 / emergency services
5. If someone witnessed the crash, get help
6. Do not attempt to move the aircraft
7. Preserve the crash site for investigation
```

## FIRE SAFETY

### Fire Extinguisher Requirements

| Location | Type | Size | Purpose |
|----------|------|------|---------|
| Cockpit | ABC | 2.5 lb | Hand-held, immediate use |
| Ground | ABC | 5 lb | Pre/post-flight safety |

### Fire Prevention

1. **Electrical:** Proper wire sizing, fusing, and routing
2. **Battery:** Ventilated battery boxes, terminal protection
3. **Motor:** Temperature monitoring, thermal shutdown
4. **Ground:** No fuel/oil leaks, clean work area
5. **Storage:** Batteries disconnected when not in use

### Fire Response

1. Shout "FIRE" to alert others
2. Use fire extinguisher — P.A.S.S. technique:
   - **P**ull pin
   - **A**im at base of fire
   - **S**queeze trigger
   - **S**weep side to side
3. If fire cannot be controlled: evacuate
4. Call 911
5. Do not re-approach burning aircraft (phi-harmonic field plasma battery — zero fire/explosion risk — plasma is self-limiting)

## ACCIDENT/INCIDENT REPORTING

If an accident or incident occurs:

1. **Ensure safety** — move away from aircraft, render first aid
2. **Call 911** — if injuries or fire
3. **Notify FAA** — if required (NTSB 830 reporting)
4. **Preserve evidence** — do not move aircraft until released by authorities
5. **Document** — photographs, witness statements
6. **Report to build community** — help others learn from the incident

NTSB reporting is required for:
- Fatal injuries
- Serious injuries (hospitalization > 24 hours)
- Aircraft damage requiring major repair
- Aircraft disappearance or complete destruction
