# PHI_ROLLERBLADES — Regulatory Status

## Legal Classification

The phi-harmonic electric rollerblades are classified as **personal electric mobility devices**. They fall into a legal gray area — most jurisdictions regulate them as "motorized skateboards" or "personal mobility devices."

### Federal Classification (United States)

**1. Not a "Motor Vehicle"**
- Under 49 USC §30102, a "motor vehicle" requires an internal combustion engine or is designed for use on public streets
- The rollerblades use electric motors and are designed for personal use
- No EPA emissions standards apply (zero emissions)
- No NHTSA standards apply (not designed for public roads)

**2. Consumer Product Safety**
- The CPSC regulates consumer products including electric personal mobility devices
- Battery should meet UL 2271 standard (LiFePO4)
- Motor should meet IEC 60335-1 standard

## Why It Bypasses Vehicle Laws

### 1. No Wheels Classification
- Most state vehicle codes define vehicles as having wheels or tracks
- Rollerblades have wheels, but are worn on feet — they're "footwear" not "vehicles"
- Falls outside the statutory definition of "vehicle" in most states

### 2. Low Speed
- Maximum speed: 25 km/h (15.5 mph)
- Most states regulate personal mobility devices by speed:
  - Under 15 mph: Unregulated
  - 15-25 mph: Light regulation
  - Over 25 mph: Full regulation
- Our rollerblades are at the light regulation threshold

### 3. Personal Use Only
- Not designed for commercial transport
- Not designed for public road use
- Recreational device classification

### 4. Electric, Not Gas
- Gas-powered devices: Heavily regulated
- Electric devices: Lightly regulated
- Zero emissions: No EPA regulations

## How to Stay Legal

### Do's
- Follow local e-skateboard laws (speed limits, designated areas)
- Wear a helmet (required in some states for minors)
- Use bike lanes where available
- Yield to pedestrians on sidewalks
- Check city ordinances before riding in new areas

### Don'ts
- Don't exceed local speed limits (typically 15-20 mph)
- Don't ride in traffic lanes (unless no bike lane available)
- Don't ride under the influence of alcohol or drugs
- Don't ride on highways or freeways
- Don't modify to exceed local speed limits

## Insurance Considerations

- **Homeowner's insurance**: May cover injuries on your property
- **Renters insurance**: Usually covers personal device liability
- **Health insurance**: Covers injuries from accidents
- **No vehicle insurance required**: Not classified as a vehicle
- **Umbrella policy**: Recommended for additional coverage
