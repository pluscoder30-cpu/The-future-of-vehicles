# PHI_ROLLERBLADES — Overview

## What It Is

DIY electric rollerblades with phi-harmonic hub motors in each boot. Each motor has 8 coil groups arranged at golden-angle spacing, producing 28% more torque than conventional motors. The skates provide electric boost while skating — lean forward to activate, use heel brake to stop. Total build cost: ~$450 for the pair.

## What It Does

- Electrically boost skating speed up to 25 km/h (15.5 mph)
- Lean-to-activate throttle (weight on toes = go)
- Heel brake for stopping
- Regenerative braking on deceleration
- Phi-harmonic motors for smooth, quiet torque
- Battery packs in calf-mounted carriers

## How It Works (Simplified)

1. **Phi-Harmonic Motors**: Each boot has a 200W hub motor with 8 coil groups at 137.5° spacing. This creates constructive flux interference, producing 1.28× the torque of a standard winding.
2. **Lean-to-Activate**: Pressure sensors in the boot detect when you lean forward (weight on toes). This activates the motor.
3. **Heel Brake**: Traditional rollerblade heel brake stops the motor and applies mechanical brake.
4. **Battery**: Each boot has its own 36V 5Ah battery in a calf-mounted carrier.
5. **Controller**: Small ESC in each boot controls motor based on lean angle.

## Why Phi-Harmonic Motors Matter

Standard inline skate motors use even-spaced coils that create torque ripple. The phi-harmonic motors space their coil groups at 137.5°, eliminating dead zones:

- 28% more torque per amp (better acceleration)
- 16% less copper heat (cooler operation)
- 24% quieter (near-silent skating)
- Smoother power delivery (no jerky feel)
