# PHI_FOLDING_EBIKE — Regulatory Status

## Legal Classification

The phi-harmonic folding e-bike is classified as a **Class 1 electric bicycle** under US federal and state law. This classification provides the most permissive legal access — allowed on roads, bike lanes, bike paths, and (in most states) sidewalks.

### Federal Classification (United States)

**1. Class 1 E-Bike**
- Under the federal e-bike definition (15 USC §2085), a Class 1 e-bike:
  - Has a motor ≤ 750W (our motor is 350W — compliant)
  - Provides pedal-assist only (our bike has pedal assist — compliant)
  - Maximum assisted speed: 20 mph (our max is 15.5 mph — compliant)
  - Has operable pedals (our bike has pedals — compliant)
- **Result**: Classified as a bicycle, not a motor vehicle

**2. Not a "Motor Vehicle"**
- Under 49 USC §30102, a "motor vehicle" requires an internal combustion engine or is designed for use on public streets
- The e-bike is classified as a bicycle under federal law
- No EPA emissions standards apply (zero emissions)
- No NHTSA standards apply (classified as bicycle)

**3. Consumer Product Safety**
- The CPSC regulates e-bikes as consumer products
- Battery should meet UL 2271 standard (LiFePO4)
- Motor should meet IEC 60335-1 standard

## Why It's Legal

### 1. Falls Within Class 1 E-Bike Category
- Commercial e-bikes (RadPower, Aventon, etc.) are legal in all 50 states
- The phi-harmonic folding e-bike has the same specifications as commercial Class 1 e-bikes
- The phi-harmonic motor is an internal design choice — no different from choosing a specific motor brand

### 2. Speed Compliant
- Maximum assisted speed: 25 km/h (15.5 mph)
- Most states allow up to 20 mph (32 km/h)
- Our e-bike is under the limit in all states

### 3. Zero Emissions
- No EPA regulations apply
- No smog check required
- No emissions testing needed

### 4. Folding Design
- Folding bikes are legal everywhere standard bikes are legal
- Can be taken on public transit (most transit systems allow folded bikes)
- Can be stored in apartments, offices, car trunks

## How to Stay Legal

### Do's
- Follow local e-bike laws (speed limits, designated areas)
- Wear a helmet (required in some states for minors)
- Use bike lanes where available
- Yield to pedestrians on sidewalks
- Check city ordinances before riding in new areas
- Register as a "personal mobility device" if your city requires it
- Use lights at night (required in all states)

### Don'ts
- Don't exceed 20 mph assisted speed
- Don't ride in traffic lanes (unless no bike lane available)
- Don't ride under the influence of alcohol or drugs
- Don't ride on highways or freeways
- Don't modify to exceed local speed limits
- Don't use commercially (deliveries, rideshare) without permits
- Don't ride without a helmet if under 18 (in states that require it)
