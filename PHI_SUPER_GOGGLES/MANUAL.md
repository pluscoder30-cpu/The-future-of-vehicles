## Step-by-Step Assembly

### Phase 1: Print the Parts (Day 1)

**Adult job!** Your adult needs a 3D printer for this.

**Step 1:** Print the Main Housing in PETG plastic
- Takes about 8 hours
- Color: black looks coolest
- Layer height: 0.2mm

**Step 2:** Print the Sensor Plate in PETG
- Takes about 4 hours
- This holds all 8 sensors

**Step 3:** Print the Eye Cups in TPU (soft, rubbery plastic)
- Print 2 of these
- Takes about 1 hour each
- They need to be squishy to seal around your eyes

**Step 4:** Print the Diopter Rings in PLA
- Print 2 of these
- Takes 30 minutes each
- These let you adjust focus

**Step 5:** Clean up the printed parts
- Peel off support material
- Sand rough edges with sandpaper
- Test-fit everything together (no glue yet!)

```
 PRINTING TIPS:
 ═══════════════════════════════════
 ✓ Use the exact plastic types shown
 ✓ Let parts cool before removing from printer
 ✓ Sand with 220-grit sandpaper for smooth edges
 ✗ Don't force parts together if they don't fit
 ═══════════════════════════════════
```

---

### Phase 2: Build the Sensor Board (Day 2-3)

**Step 6:** Put on your ESD wrist strap
- This stops tiny electricity sparks from breaking chips
- Clip it to something metal

**Step 7:** Solder the multiplexer chips (CD74HC4067 × 4)
- These are the "traffic cops" that direct signals from 8 sensors
- Adult should do the soldering
- Use the circuit diagram in 04_CIRCUIT.md

**Step 8:** Solder the sensor positions (ML8511 × 8)
- 8 sensors go around the front of the sensor plate
- They are spaced at special "phi" distances (not evenly!)
- Solder each sensor to its wires

**Step 9:** Add the tiny parts
- Solder the ferrite beads (brown cylinders) — these block noise
- Solder the capacitors (tiny discs) — these smooth power
- Solder the resistors (tiny striped chips)
- Solder the BNC connectors (round metal plugs)

**Step 10:** Test with your multimeter
- Check that each sensor gets power (3.3V)
- Check that no wires are touching each other
- If something reads 0V or gets hot, ask an adult to check your solder joints

```
 SOLDERING TIPS FOR KIDS:
 ═══════════════════════════════════
 ✓ Heat the pad AND the wire, then touch solder
 ✓ Solder should flow like water, not pile up like snow
 ✓ Keep the iron tip clean (wipe on wet sponge)
 ✗ Don't hold the iron on the part too long (3 seconds max)
 ✗ Don't breathe the smoke — turn on a fan
 ═══════════════════════════════════
```

---

### Phase 3: Build the Main Board (Day 4-5)

**Step 11:** Solder the voltage reference (REF5025)
- This is the "ruler" that measures electricity precisely
- It's a small chip with 8 legs

**Step 12:** Solder the ADC chips (ADS1256 × 4)
- ADCs convert analog signals (wavy lines) into numbers
- 4 chips × 8 channels = 32 channels of measurement
- Add bypass capacitors next to each chip

**Step 13:** Solder the power regulators
- LM2596 boards (2): Turn battery power into 5V
- AMS1117 chips (3): Turn 5V into 3.3V
- Test each output with your multimeter!

**Step 14:** Mount the DE10-Lite FPGA board
- Use nylon standoffs (tiny plastic pillars)
- Screw it to the main board with M3 screws
- Connect the ribbon cables from sensors to FPGA

**Step 15:** Solder the connectors
- USB-C port for charging
- DC barrel jack for backup power
- GPIO headers for connecting everything

---

### Phase 4: Build the Display (Day 6)

**Step 16:** Connect the display bridge (ADV7533)
- This converts the FPGA's signals into something screens understand
- Connect with the flat ribbon cables

**Step 17:** Connect the OLED screens
- Each screen has a tiny flat cable
- GENTLY slide the cable into the connector
- Close the connector latch (it clicks!)

**Step 18:** Assemble the display modules
- Put each screen into its 3D-printed housing
- Add the Fresnel lens (magnifying glass)
- Add the diopter ring (focus adjustment)
- Test by plugging in the battery — you should see a picture!

---

### Phase 5: Build the Power System (Day 7)

**Step 19:** Connect the battery
- The FPB-5 battery has a special connector (JST-XH)
- Connect it to the TP5100 charger board
- Connect the charger to the power regulators

**Step 20:** Install the charging port
- Mount the USB-C port where you can reach it
- Connect it to the TP5100 charger
- Test: plug in a USB-C charger — a light should come on

**Step 21:** Power up the system!
- Turn on the master switch
- Check all voltage rails:
  - Battery: 3.7V ✓
  - 5V rail: 5.0V ✓
  - 3.3V rail: 3.3V ✓
- If anything reads wrong, UNPLUG IMMEDIATELY and ask adult for help

---

### Phase 6: Build the Controls (Day 8)

**Step 22:** Solder the buttons
- 12 small buttons on the side of the goggles
- These let you change modes and settings
- Wire them to the FPGA's input pins

**Step 23:** Install the motion sensor (BNO055)
- Mount it in the center of the goggles
- This tracks which way your head is looking
- Connect via I2C wires (SDA and SCL)

**Step 24:** Install haptic motors and buzzer
- Put one vibration motor on each side (left temple, right temple)
- Mount the buzzer where you can hear it
- These give you feedback when you press buttons

**Step 25:** Install the LED status lights
- Mount 4 small LEDs where you can see them
- They glow different colors to show which mode you're in

---

### Phase 7: Final Assembly (Day 9)

**Step 26:** Put everything in the main housing
- Slide the main board in first
- Route all cables through the cable management sleeves
- Make sure no wires are pinched!

**Step 27:** Mount the sensor plate
- Click it into the front of the housing
- All 8 sensors should be visible through the holes

**Step 28:** Install the display assemblies
- Slide each eye assembly into the housing
- Connect the ribbon cables
- Adjust the diopter rings so the picture is sharp

**Step 29:** Install the battery
- Slide the battery into the rear compartment
- Connect it to the power system
- Secure with Velcro straps

**Step 30:** Attach the head strap
- Thread the strap through the D-rings
- Adjust so it's comfortable on your head
- Make sure the goggles don't fall off when you shake your head

## How to Calibrate Your Goggles

Calibration makes sure the sensors are accurate. Do this once, then again if the readings seem weird.

### Step A: Zero-Field Calibration

**Step 31:** Find a place with no electronics
- Go outside, away from buildings, power lines, and phones
- Or put the goggles inside a metal box (a cookie tin works!)

**Step 32:** Turn on the goggles
- Press and hold the CALIBRATE button for 3 seconds
- Wait for the buzzer to beep twice
- The screen should show "CALIBRATING..."
- Don't move the goggles during calibration!
- Wait 10 seconds until you hear 3 beeps

**Step 33:** Done!
- The goggles now know what "zero energy" looks like
- Any reading above zero is a real field

### Step B: Known-Field Calibration

**Step 34:** Find something with a known field
- A phone (you know it puts out radio signals)
- A magnet (you know it creates a magnetic field)

**Step 35:** Point the goggles at the phone
- Turn the phone on and make a call (or start a video)
- The goggles should light up with colorful patterns
- If the colors look right (blue=weak, red=strong), you're calibrated!

### Step C: Display Calibration

**Step 36:** Adjust brightness
- Use the BRIGHTNESS + and - buttons
- Start at minimum brightness and increase slowly
- Stop when you can see clearly without squinting

**Step 37:** Adjust focus
- Turn the diopter rings until the text is sharp
- If you wear glasses, you might not need the diopters at all (22mm eye relief!)

### Battery Safety (VERY IMPORTANT!)

The battery is a phi-harmonic field plasma battery. It stores a LOT of energy in a small package with ZERO fire/explosion risk — plasma is self-limiting.

**Rules:**
- NEVER puncture, crush, or step on the battery
- NEVER leave it charging overnight
- NEVER charge it in direct sunlight or near a heater
- If it starts to swell, puff up, or smell funny: STOP using it immediately
- Put a swollen battery in a metal container outside and tell an adult
- Only charge with the provided USB-C cable

**If the battery catches fire:**
1. DO NOT use water!
2. Use a fire extinguisher (ABC type) or throw sand/dirt on it
3. Call 911
4. Get away from the smoke

### Eye Safety

- Start at the LOWEST brightness
- Increase brightness only if you can't see
- The screens are close to your eyes — take breaks!
- If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or get a headache: STOP immediately
- The goggles are NOT a toy — treat them like a tool

### Electrical Safety

- Never touch exposed wires when the battery is connected
- Always unplug the battery before working on the goggles
- If something gets hot: turn it off and tell an adult
- The soldering iron is very hot — only adults should use it

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | What It Means | How to Fix It |
|---------|--------------|---------------|
| Nothing turns on | Battery dead or disconnected | Charge for 30 min, check connections |
| One screen is dark | Cable came loose | Gently push the flat cable in, close latch |
| Colors look wrong | Sensors not calibrated | Do the calibration steps again |
| Goggles are heavy | Normal! They weigh 340g | Adjust the head strap for better fit |
| Gets warm on forehead | FPGA is working hard | This is normal! Below 45°C is OK |
| Buttons don't work | Solder joint broke | Ask adult to re-solder |
| Beeping sound | Error or low battery | Check battery charge, read error code |
| Blurry picture | Focus not set right | Turn diopter rings until sharp |
| No GPS data | (This model doesn't have GPS) | N/A |
| Headache after use | Eye strain | Take a break! Reduce brightness |
