# PHI_GLIDER — Regulatory Status

## Legal Classification

The phi-harmonic glider is classified as an **ultralight aircraft** in most jurisdictions. Here's how it fits into existing laws:

### Federal Classification (United States)

**1. Ultralight Category (FAR Part 103)**
- Maximum speed: 55 km/h (34 mph) — **EXCEEDS LIMIT** (our glider: 40 km/h)
- Maximum gross weight: 115 kg (254 lbs) — **EXCEEDS LIMIT** (our glider: 105 kg)
- **NOT eligible for ultralight category**

**2. Experimental Amateur-Built Category**
- Must be built by amateur for education/recreation
- Must not be for commercial use
- Requires registration as Experimental Amateur-Built
- Requires pilot certificate (Sport Pilot minimum)
- Requires medical certificate (Sport Pilot minimum)

**3. Light Sport Aircraft Category**
- Maximum speed: 222 km/h (120 knots)
- Maximum gross weight: 600 kg (1320 lbs)
- Requires Sport Pilot certificate
- Requires medical certificate (Sport Pilot minimum)

### State-Level Classification

Most states defer to federal aviation regulations (FAA):
- **No state-specific laws** for ultralights
- **Federal rules apply** in all states
- **Local ordinances** may restrict where you can fly

## Why It's Legal (With Restrictions)

### 1. Experimental Amateur-Built
- Can be built by amateur for education/recreation
- Must not be for commercial use
- **Requires registration and pilot certificate**

### 2. No Commercial Use
- Cannot be used for hire or reward
- Cannot carry passengers for compensation
- Cannot be used for delivery or transport

### 3. Restricted Airspace
- Cannot fly in controlled airspace (Class B, C, D)
- Cannot fly over congested areas
- Cannot fly within 5 miles of airports (without permission)

## How to Stay Legal

### Do's
✅ Register with FAA as Experimental Amateur-Built
✅ Get Sport Pilot certificate (minimum)
✅ Get medical certificate (Sport Pilot minimum)
✅ Get insurance
✅ Fly in uncontrolled airspace only
✅ Maintain minimum altitudes
✅ Stay away from airports and heliports

### Don'ts
❌ Don't fly in controlled airspace without permission
❌ Don't fly over congested areas
❌ Don't fly within 5 miles of airports without permission
❌ Don't fly at night without special equipment
❌ Don't fly in instrument meteorological conditions (IMC)
❌ Don't fly under the influence of alcohol or drugs
❌ Don't fly commercially

## Registration Requirements

### United States
1. **Register with FAA** as Experimental Amateur-Built
2. **Apply for airworthiness certificate** (limited)
3. **Get pilot certificate** (Sport Pilot minimum)
4. **Get medical certificate** (Sport Pilot minimum)
5. **Get insurance** (required in most states)

### International
- **Canada**: Register with Transport Canada
- **United Kingdom**: Register with CAA
- **Germany**: Register with LBA
- **France**: Register with DGAC
- **Australia**: Register with CASA

- Over other areas: 500 ft above surface
- **Always maintain safe altitude for emergency landing**

## Phi-Harmonic Motor: Legal Nuance

The phi-harmonic motor arrangement is **functionally identical** to standard ducted fans for legal purposes:

- Same power rating (400W total)
- Same classification (Experimental Amateur-Built)
- Same pilot requirements (Sport Pilot minimum)

The phi-harmonic geometry is an **internal design improvement** — it doesn't change the legal classification. It's like using better bearings in an aircraft — still just an aircraft.
