# PHI SYNTHETIC WINGS — Design Principles

## Biomimetic Flight Theory
Bird wings generate lift through flapping motion that creates pressure differentials above and below the wing. The Synthetic Wings replicate this motion with mechanical wing structures, then amplify the lift force by 100x using phi-harmonic field coupling.

## The Lift Amplification Equation
```
L_total = L_mechanical × (1 + Q × sin(φ × ω × t))²
```
Where:
- L_total = total lift force
- L_mechanical = mechanical lift from flapping
- Q = quality factor of phi-harmonic resonance
- φ = golden ratio
- ω = wing beat angular frequency

At Q=10, this achieves 100x mechanical lift amplification.

## Core Design Tenets

### 1. Augment, Don't Replace
The wings work with the human body, not against it. Flapping motion is controlled by the wearer's arm movements. The phi-harmonic system amplifies this natural motion.

### 2. Three-Axis Control
- **Pitch:** Arm position (forward/back)
- **Roll:** Arm height differential (left/right)
- **Yaw:** Wrist rotation
- **Hover:** Arms at neutral position

### 3. Safety First
The wings include a ballistic parachute, automatic descent on power failure, and hover-at-will capability. No flight training required for basic operation.

### 4. Foldable Design
Wings fold flat against the back for ground transport. Deployment takes 10 seconds. Total weight with battery: 12 kg.

## Aesthetic Language
- **Form:** Feathered wing geometry (5m wingspan)
- **Color:** White with golden phi-trace patterns
- **Texture:** Flexible polymer "feathers" over carbon frame
- **Illumination:** Golden glow from phi-harmonic emitters during flight
- **Movement:** Organic, bird-like flapping motion
