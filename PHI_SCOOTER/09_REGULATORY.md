# PHI_SCOOTER — Regulatory Status

## Legal Classification

The phi-harmonic scooter is classified as an **electric scooter** in most jurisdictions. Here's how it fits into existing laws:

### Federal Classification (United States)

**1. Not a "Motor Vehicle"**
- Under federal law, electric scooters under 750W and 20 mph are **not motor vehicles**
- No registration, insurance, or driver's license required
- No EPA emissions standards apply
- No NHTSA standards apply

**2. State-Level Classification**
Most states have adopted electric scooter laws:

| State | Legal Status | Where Allowed | Age Requirement |
|-------|--------------|---------------|-----------------|
| California | Legal | Bike lanes, paths | 16+ |
| Texas | Legal | Same as bicycle | None specified |
| New York | Legal | Bike lanes | 16+ |
| Florida | Legal | Same as bicycle | None specified |
| Illinois | Legal | Same as bicycle | None specified |
| Washington | Legal | Bike lanes, paths | 16+ |
| Oregon | Legal | Bike lanes, paths | 16+ |

**Most states treat electric scooters similarly to bicycles**

## Why It's Legal

### 1. Meets Electric Scooter Requirements
- Motor power: 350W (under 750W limit)
- Max speed: 25 km/h (under most limits)
- **Fully compliant with electric scooter laws**

### 2. No Registration Required
- Most states don't require registration for electric scooters
- No license plate needed
- Just ride it like a kick scooter

### 3. No Insurance Required
- Most states don't require insurance for electric scooters
- Check local laws — some cities may require it
- Homeowner's insurance may cover liability

### 4. No Special Parking
- Can park at any bicycle rack
- Can bring on public transit (most systems)
- Can store in apartment buildings (most allow scooters)

## How to Stay Legal

### Do's
✅ Ride in bike lanes when available
✅ Follow all traffic laws (same as bicycle)
✅ Wear a helmet (required under 16-18 in most places)
✅ Use lights at night (front white, rear red)
✅ Yield to pedestrians
✅ Signal turns and stops

### Don'ts
❌ Don't ride on sidewalks in business districts (most cities prohibit)
❌ Don't ride under the influence of alcohol or drugs
❌ Don't modify to exceed speed limits
❌ Don't carry passengers (single-rider only)
❌ Don't ride in pedestrian-only zones
❌ Don't block bike lanes when parked

## Phi-Harmonic Motor: Legal Nuance

The phi-harmonic motor arrangement is **functionally identical** to a standard hub motor for legal purposes:

- Same power rating (350W)
- Same speed limit (25 km/h)
- Same classification (electric scooter)

The phi-harmonic geometry is an **internal design improvement** — it doesn't change the legal classification. It's like using better bearings in a scooter — still just a scooter.
