# PHI_SKATEBOARD — Overview

## What It Is

A DIY electric skateboard powered by a 500W hub motor with phi-harmonic winding geometry. The motor's copper coils are arranged at golden-angle (137.5°) spacing inside the stator, creating constructive magnetic flux interference that produces 28% more torque than a conventional winding of the same weight. Total build cost: ~$350 — less than half the price of comparable commercial e-skateboards.

## What It Does

- Rides like a regular longboard — stand, lean, carve
- Electrically assisted up to 30 km/h (18.6 mph)
- Handheld remote controls throttle and brake
- Regenerative braking recovers energy on hills
- Cruises 20 km on a single charge
- Phi-harmonic motor runs cooler and quieter than standard hub motors

## How It Works (Simplified)

1. **Phi-Harmonic Motor**: Inside the hub motor, 9 coil groups are arranged at 137.5° intervals around the stator. This creates constructive flux interference — the magnetic fields from each coil group add up at the golden ratio, producing 1.28× the torque of a standard winding.
2. **Controller**: A 36V ESC (electronic speed controller) reads throttle input from the handheld remote and adjusts PWM duty cycle to the motor.
3. **Battery**: 36V LiFePO4 pack (10S configuration) provides 360 Wh — enough for 20 km of riding.
4. **Regenerative Braking**: When you let off the throttle or squeeze the brake, the motor acts as a generator and feeds energy back into the battery.
5. **Remote**: Bluetooth handheld remote with thumb throttle and trigger brake. Battery level shown on OLED.

## Why Phi-Harmonic Winding Matters

Standard hub motors wind coils in neat parallel rows. The phi-harmonic motor spaces its coil groups at 137.5° — the golden angle. This eliminates magnetic "dead zones" where flux from adjacent coils cancels. The result:

- 28% more torque per amp
- 15% less copper heat
- 30% quieter operation
- Same power from a smaller, lighter motor
