# PHI_SKATEBOARD — Regulatory Status

## Legal Classification

The phi-harmonic electric skateboard is classified as a **personal electric mobility device**, similar to commercial electric skateboards. It's largely unregulated because it falls within existing e-skateboard categories.

### Federal Classification (United States)

**1. Electric Skateboard Classification**
- Under most federal frameworks, electric skateboards are classified as "personal mobility devices"
- No EPA emissions standards apply (zero emissions)
- No NHTSA standards apply (not designed for public roads)
- CPSC may apply to the battery (UL 2271 certification recommended)

**2. Not a "Motor Vehicle"**
- Under 49 USC §30102, a "motor vehicle" requires an internal combustion engine or is designed for use on public streets
- The skateboard uses an electric motor and is designed for personal transportation
- Falls under "low-speed electric bicycle" exemptions in most states

**3. Consumer Product Safety**
- The CPSC regulates consumer products including electric skateboards
- Battery should meet UL 2271 standard (LiFePO4)
- Motor should meet IEC 60335-1 standard

## Why It's Legal

### 1. Falls Within E-Skateboard Category
- Commercial e-skateboards (Boosted, Meepo, etc.) are legal in all 50 states
- The phi-harmonic skateboard has the same form factor and performance
- The phi-harmonic motor is an internal design choice — no different from using a belt-drive vs. hub motor

### 2. Speed Compliant
- Maximum speed: 30 km/h (18.6 mph)
- Most states allow up to 20 mph (32 km/h)
- Our skateboard is under the limit in all states

### 3. Zero Emissions
- No EPA regulations apply
- No smog check required
- No emissions testing needed

### 4. Private Property Use
- Vehicle laws apply to public roads and sidewalks
- On private property, you can build and ride any device you want
- Similar to go-karts, ATVs, or dirt bikes used only on private land

## How to Stay Legal

### Do's
- Follow local e-skateboard laws (speed limits, designated areas)
- Wear a helmet (required in some states for minors)
- Use bike lanes where available
- Yield to pedestrians on sidewalks
- Check city ordinances before riding in new areas
- Register as a "personal mobility device" if your city requires it

### Don'ts
- Don't exceed local speed limits (typically 15-20 mph)
- Don't ride in traffic lanes (unless local law allows)
- Don't ride under the influence of alcohol or drugs
- Don't ride on highways or freeways
- Don't modify to exceed local speed limits
- Don't use commercially (deliveries, rideshare) without permits

## Insurance Considerations

- **Homeowner's insurance**: May cover injuries on your property
- **Renters insurance**: Usually covers personal device liability
- **Health insurance**: Covers injuries from accidents
- **No vehicle insurance required**: Not classified as a vehicle
- **Umbrella policy**: Recommended for additional coverage
