# PHI CHEAP SHUTTLE — REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

## FAA/AST Regulatory Pathway

---

## REGULATORY CLASSIFICATION

The PHI Cheap Shuttle falls under two regulatory frameworks:

1. **FAA Part 103 — Ultralight Vehicles** (for ground testing/taxi only)
2. **FAA Part 21.191 — Experimental Amateur-Built** (for flight)
3. **FAA/AST — Commercial Space Transportation** (for suborbital launch)

---

## PATH 1: ULTRALIGHT (Ground Testing Only)

### Requirements (14 CFR Part 103)
- Empty weight: ≤ 254 kg (560 lb) — **FAIL: 202.5 kg (OK for ground testing)**
- Max speed: ≤ 55 knots (102 km/h) — **FAIL for flight, OK for taxi**
- Max fuel capacity: 19L (5 gal) — **N/A (electric)**
- No passenger carrying for compensation — **OK**
- Single occupant — **FAIL: 2-seat**

**Verdict:** Part 103 does not apply for flight operations. Can be used for taxi testing only.

---

## PATH 2: EXPERIMENTAL AMATEUR-BUILT

### Requirements (14 CFR Part 21.191)
1. Must be assembled by applicant (major portion rule: >51% by person seeking certificate)
2. Must be for education/recreation
3. Must pass condition inspection
4. Must receive airworthiness certificate

### Application Process

**Step 1: Aircraft Registration (FAA Form 8050-1)**
- File with FAA Aircraft Registration Branch
- Fee: $5.00
- Requires bill of materials showing amateur-built status
- Timeline: 6-8 weeks

**Step 2: Airworthiness Certificate (FAA Form 8130-6)**
- Submit to local FSDO (Flight Standards District Office)
- Schedule inspection with Designated Airworthiness Representative (DAR)
- Required documentation:
  - Weight and balance report
  - Equipment list
  - Operating limitations
  - Flight test plan
- Fee: $0 (no fee for experimental)
- Timeline: 2-4 weeks after registration

**Step 3: Operating Limitations**
- Restricted to:
  - Visual flight rules (VFR) only
  - Within 30 nm of home base (first 25 hours)
  - No flight over densely populated areas
  - No carrying of passengers for compensation
  - Not for hire

**Step 4: Flight Test Phase**
- First 25 hours: Solo only (pilot only, no passengers)
- Must be within 30 nm of home base
- After 25 hours: Can carry passengers
- After 40 hours: Can apply for standard airworthiness (optional)

## PATH 3: SUBORBITAL LAUNCH (FAA/AST)

### Requirements (14 CFR Part 400)
For any launch exceeding 50,000 ft (15.24 km), FAA AST (Office of Commercial Space Transportation) authorization is required.

### Launch License Application

**Step 1: Pre-Application Consultation**
- Contact FAA AST: (202) 267-7600
- Submit pre-application notice
- Discuss vehicle classification and safety
- Timeline: 30-60 days

**Step 2: Application (FAA Form 8430-1)**
- Submit launch license application
- Required documentation:
  - Vehicle description and specifications
  - Safety analysis (hazard report)
  - Flight trajectory analysis
  - Public risk assessment
  - Insurance certificate ($5M minimum)
  - Environmental review (NEPA)
  - Launch site license
- Fee: $5,000 (application) + $2,500/year (license)
- Timeline: 6-18 months

**Step 3: Safety Review**
- FAA reviews hazard report
- Conducts independent trajectory analysis
- Reviews public risk assessment
- Maximum allowable casualty risk: 1 × 10⁻⁶ per launch

**Step 4: License Issuance**
- Valid for 5 years
- Annual reporting required
- Must comply with all operating conditions

## ENVIRONMENTAL COMPLIANCE

### NEPA (National Environmental Policy Act)
- Required for FAA/AST license
- Categorical exclusion possible for:
  - Launches < 4 suborbital flights/year
  - No significant noise impact
  - No hazardous materials
- Timeline: 30-60 days

### FFC Field (Field Frequency Commission)
- Required for all radio transmissions
- Frequencies used:
  - VHF (136-174 MHz): License-free (Part 95)
  - 433 MHz telemetry: License-free (FFC Field Standards 15)
  - GPS L1 (1575.42 MHz): Receive-only (no license needed)
- FFC Field ID required for all transmitting devices

## STATE AND LOCAL REGULATIONS

### State Requirements (vary by state)
- Some states require additional permits for rocket/space launches
- Environmental impact assessments may be required
- Local zoning may restrict launch site location

### Insurance Requirements
- Minimum $5M liability (FAA/AST requirement)
- Recommended: $10M product liability
- Estimated annual premium: $5,000-$15,000
